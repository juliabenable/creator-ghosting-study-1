08

The edge-case audit — all 31, before polish

31 cases · 6 groups · tagged by status

Organized by where in the lifecycle each one bites. Decided means the Aug 18 call or the fairness spec already answers it — polish just has to implement the answer. Needs decision is genuinely open — flagged for you, Tony, or Katie by name. Eng / Ops / Legal are execution details that will bite if nobody owns them.

A

Before the screen — who sees it at all

5 cases
E-1 · The A/B split leaksops

Half of creators get the card step, half don't — and creators talk. Katie needs a one-line answer for "why me?": "We're rolling the Content Guarantee out gradually — everyone will have it soon." True, calm, no fairness debate.

E-2 · Proven-creator carve-outdecidedops

Tony: repeat creators with clean records skip the screen. Needs a hard rule, not vibes — e.g. 2+ completed campaigns, zero charge-eligible incidents — so Katie applies it consistently and the A/B stays clean.

E-3 · Minorslegal

A card agreement with a 17-year-old is voidable. If any creators are under 18 (ToS minimum is 18 — verify enforcement), the card arm must exclude them at eligibility, not at the charge.

E-4 · Experience campaignsneeds decision

"$62 product cost" works for serums. For hotel-stay collabs (Trilogy-style), the "product" is a room night — is that chargeable, at what value, and does a no-show change the trigger? Recommend: exclude experience campaigns from the card arm for now.

E-5 · Des's collectivedecided

The beauty-sub cohort gets its own messaging and should ghost near 0% — keep them out of the A/B so their numbers don't pollute either arm.

B

On the screen — adding the card

6 cases
E-6 · Apple Pay for a future chargeeng

Saving via Apple Pay must run as a Stripe SetupIntent with off_session usage — and some issuers decline later merchant-initiated charges on tokenized cards. Works, but test the full save-then-charge loop in staging before trusting the AP path; hide the button where Apple Pay is unavailable (Android/web) and consider Google Pay parity.

E-7 · Card declined at saveeng

Failed SetupIntent (bad number, expired, AVS/ZIP mismatch, 3DS challenge) needs inline error states + retry. EU-issued cards may force a 3DS sheet — handle it at save time so the future charge is exempt, never at charge time.

E-8 · Prepaid & virtual cardsneeds decision

A Privacy.com virtual card can be killed seconds after saving — the hold becomes theater. Options: block prepaid BINs (Stripe exposes card funding type), or accept it because the device is psychological anyway. Recommend: accept, don't block — blocking adds friction for honest people to stop a fraction who'd ghost anyway.

E-9 · The stalldecidedops

Creator reaches the screen and doesn't finish. Decided: order stands, Katie emails the waiver ("we've removed that step for you") after a stall window. Polish detail: define the window (24h), and make sure the reminder ladder doesn't fire "add your card" texts after the waiver.

E-10 · Back-button and re-entryeng

Creator hits ‹ from the card step, or kills the app mid-entry: product selection must stay confirmed, and re-entering the portal should land them back on the card step (not a dead end, not a duplicate order).

E-11 · The sheet's reply channelsops

The sheet invites questions to kscalzo@ and a text line. Both must be monitored, and any inbound on either counts as "a reply from you" — the charge trigger's "no reply" clause dies the moment they use the contact info we gave them.

C

While the campaign runs — the card sits

6 cases
E-12 · The amount must track the selectioneng

"$62 for this campaign" is consent language. If product sets have unequal values or the creator swaps options after saving, the disclosed amount is stale — recompute and re-disclose (a quiet portal line + email: "your Content Guarantee amount is now $48").

E-13 · Charge > disclosed amount is never OKlegal

Card-network rules for merchant-initiated charges require the charge to match the agreed terms. Charge exactly the disclosed number or less, never more — shipping, tax, and "admin fees" stay out (the StubHub $25 fee is where their goodwill dies; we decided no fees, ever).

E-14 · Card expires or is canceled mid-campaigneng

Saved in August, expires September, campaign closes October. Stripe's card-account-updater catches most reissues on network tokens; still need a "card no longer valid" state — and a decision to not nag for a replacement (the hold's job is mostly done by then; a nag reads as "we're planning to charge you").

E-15 · Creator wants the card removedneeds decision

Mid-campaign "please delete my card" request. Options: allow (hold is consent-based; refusing looks hostage-y) with Katie noting the account, or replace-only while a campaign is live. Recommend: allow removal via Katie, treat it as a churn-risk signal, never as a fight.

E-16 · Backing out of the campaigndecided

Decided on the call: backing out after product selection is fine, card or no card — the order may still ship (Shopify automation), and we eat it. Polish: the release email ("your card was never charged and has been released") must fire on any exit path.

E-17 · Extensions pause everythingdecided

An accepted extension moves the deadline — the warning clock, the charge eligibility, and the reminder ladder all recompute from the new date. One granted extension also voids "no reply" (they replied to get it).

D

The warning & the charge — Katie's moment

6 cases
E-18 · "Posted but never told us"ops

The known #1 false positive: content is live, link never submitted (the auto-detect fix is in NewSurge's backlog). Until it ships, the final warning IS the safety net — its copy must say "already posted? just reply with the link." Katie checks socials manually before any charge.

E-19 · Draft in review ≠ chargeabledecided

Fairness spec: a late draft sitting in review is never chargeable — the failure is ours or the brand's, not theirs. The trigger requires nothing submitted, not nothing published.

E-20 · TCPA on the warning textlegal

The final warning goes "by text and email" — but automated texts only to creators who ticked the SMS box. For non-opted-in creators: email + DM, and the sheet's promise should hold ("text and email" is fine — Katie can send the text manually, which is the carve-out Tony's confirming).

E-21 · Warning timing & holidaysdecidedops

From the call: don't berate people on Christmas. The warning window pauses over major holidays and fires on weekdays; the 72h grace never expires on a Sunday. (Full spec below.)

E-22 · The charge failseng

Insufficient funds, killed virtual card, closed account. One retry after 3 days, then stop — no dunning ladder, no collections. Account consequence (no future campaigns) replaces the money. We are not a debt collector; the write-off is the cost of the filter.

E-23 · Only Katie chargesdecidedops

No cron job ever charges a card. The charge is a human action with a checklist (fairness spec printed as UI), an audit log entry, and a receipt email sent the same minute. Two-person rule for the first ten charges.

E

After the charge — money in motion

4 cases
E-24 · Post-and-refunddecided

Post within 7 days of the charge → full refund, automatic, no negotiation. The receipt email leads with this: the goal was always the content, not the $62. Refund SLA: same-day initiation.

E-25 · Chargebackslegaleng

Some creators will dispute instead of replying. Evidence pack per charge, exported before filing: the saved-card consent screen, the sheet copy, contact-attempt timeline, the final warning, delivery confirmation. Budget to lose some anyway — and treat a dispute as relationship-over, not a fight to win.

E-26 · The brand's side of the moneyops

Call decision: we reimburse the brand (MD Solar) when we don't charge; when we do charge, the $62 passes through. Needs a ledger line per incident so wrap-up reporting and Katie's mental math agree.

E-27 · The relationship afterneeds decision

Charged creator wants back in next quarter. Blocked forever, cooling-off, or clean slate after one completed campaign? Recommend: cooling-off + Katie's discretion — the charge already settled the debt; permanent exile turns restitution back into punishment.

F

Copy & comprehension — the quiet failures

4 cases
E-28 · "Rare case" must stay trueops

Tony's copy promises charges are rare. If the A/B works, they will be (<2% of creators ever ghosted). Track it: the day charges stop being rare, the copy — and the program — is broken upstream.

E-29 · The placeholder phone numberneeds decision

(XXX) XXX-XXXX needs a real number before ship — presumably the existing SMS line — and inbound texts to it must be visible to Katie and logged as replies (see E-11).

E-30 · Screenshot-abilityops

This screen will be screenshotted into creator group chats. Read 4·A cold as a skeptic: every sentence should survive being posted without context. (Tony's draft does — "you'll always receive a final warning first" is the line that defuses the thread.)

E-31 · The word "guarantee" cuts both wayslegal

"Content Guarantee" is a promise to brands. Brand-side copy must not overpromise ("guaranteed content from every creator") — we guarantee the system (card-backed commitment + make-goods), not each individual post. Legal pass on brand-facing use of the phrase.

Campaign closed + product delivered + nothing submitted + no reply on any channel across the whole campaign (text, email, DM — E-11 channels included).

What StubHub actually says — the voice for receipts & warnings

"process, not personal," dissected

Their seller-penalty copy, move by move

  1. 1Flat conditional grammar, no adjectives. The page opens by listing the trigger events plainly — fail to deliver the tickets you sold, deliver wrong or invalid tickets, change a listing after sale. If X, then Y. No "unfortunately," no blame words.
  2. 2The failure is priced, precisely. Two named recovery paths — the cost of replacement tickets for the buyer, or a 40% deduction from the sale — plus a $25 administrative fee per order. Numbers, not vibes: you can compute your worst case before you sell.
  3. 3The money has a destination. Charges are framed as covering the cost of making the buyer whole (their FanProtect guarantee: the buyer gets comparable-or-better replacement tickets). It's restitution with a receipt, not a punishment.
  4. 4Collection is mechanical. The charge goes to the payment method on the seller's account, and unpaid amounts offset future payouts. Stated as plumbing, not threat.
  5. 5Agreed in advance. All of it lives in seller terms the seller accepted at listing time — which is why Tony feels "upset at the process, not at them" when it fires.
The one line worth quoting on the replacement path: penalties can include "a comparable replacement ticket (which can exceed the original ticket price)."StubHub UK seller support, fetched Aug 18, 2026 — full text at support.stubhub.co.uk, article 80001014186

What we borrow vs. soften

  1. ✓Borrow: if-then grammar, the priced worst case ($62, once), the money's destination ("covers the brand's actual cost" — Tony's sheet already does this), agreed-in-advance framing.
  2. ✓Borrow: mechanical collection language in the receipt email — timeline of facts, no adjectives.
  3. ~Soften: StubHub has no mercy door; we add the final warning (Katie's edit), post-and-refund, and "one reply is always enough" — because our relationship is repeat-collaboration, theirs is transactional.
  4. ~Soften: no administrative fee, ever — Tony's "with no additional fees" line is the anti-StubHub move. The charge is the product cost exactly, or it's nothing.
