Are we covered to launch the card feature?

The next campaigns will ask creators to save a credit card that Benable can, in rare cases, charge. This page answers one question: do our current Terms of Use and Privacy Policy cover that, or does something need to ship first? Short answer: the Privacy Policy is fine as is, the Terms of Use are not — and the fix is one short page, already drafted.

For: the launch decision Verified against: Terms of Use full text · live Privacy Policy · backend code Fix drafted: Content Guarantee Terms v1
§1

The feature, in sixty seconds

Skip ahead if you know the card screen. For everyone else:

the featureThe Content Guarantee

In gifted campaigns, brands ship free product expecting a post, and some creators keep the product and disappear. To fix that, campaign signup will now include a card screen: the creator saves a credit or debit card, and nothing is charged at save, or ever on the happy path. A charge can only happen if a campaign closes with the product kept, nothing posted, and no reply from the creator, always after a final warning naming the amount and date. Even then, the charge is the product's cost only (for example $62), and posting within 7 days of a charge refunds it in full. The card exists to deter ghosting, not to make money: the plan is to almost never charge anyone.

§2

The answer

We read both governing documents in full against this feature. One covers it, one doesn't.

terms of useNot covered

The Terms of Use that creators accept today never mention charging anyone's card, for anything. The only money language in the whole document says the app is free but might charge for its services someday — that covers subscription-style fees, not taking $62 from a saved card after a campaign goes wrong. So if we charged a creator today, no document they have agreed to authorizes that charge.

What closes the gap: a one-page terms document for this feature (see §4 — it is already drafted). We do not need to edit the main Terms of Use, and no existing user has to re-accept anything: the main Terms already contain a clause saying that individual features can come with their own additional terms. This new page simply becomes that additional-terms page for the card feature.

privacy policyCovered

The Privacy Policy already discloses everything this feature does with data: it says Benable collects payment card details through a payment processor (card type, last 4 digits, billing address, transaction history) and lists payment processors among the parties data is shared with. Collecting and storing the card is therefore already disclosed. Nothing needs to ship before launch.

One sentence to tighten later: the policy currently says card details are collected "to process your payment," which describes buying something. Ours is a card kept on file that is usually never charged. Extending that sentence with "or to keep a payment method on file" makes the purpose exact. Not a blocker — batch it with the SMS wording update already planned for these documents.

So, are we covered?Privacy Policy: yes, launch as is. Terms of Use: no — before the first campaign shows the card screen, publish the one-page Content Guarantee Terms and link it from that screen. That is the whole gap.
§3

What the gap costs, if we ever charge without fixing it

The Terms gap only matters at the moment we actually charge someone. Since the design already warns first, cancels on any reply, and refunds if the creator posts within 7 days, here is the honest severity ladder for charging without a terms page — from the usual case to the worst.

Nothing happens

The overwhelming case. The card deters, the content ships or the card step is waived, nobody is charged. The gap never surfaces.

likelihoodAlmost always

A charged creator disputes the charge with their bank

Charged people tend to call their bank, not us — so the first we hear of it is the bank demanding proof that the cardholder agreed to this kind of charge. Without a terms page, our best evidence is a screenshot of an app screen that may have changed since. That makes the dispute a coin flip: losing costs the $62 back plus a roughly $15 dispute fee. Annoying, but capped and survivable.

per chargeThe common bad path

Too many lost disputes put our payment account at risk

Payment processors score every business by disputes as a share of its charges. Benable charges cards rarely, so even two or three disputes make that share look alarming. An elevated score triggers monitoring and review; the far end is a restricted payment account — which would break card processing for everything Benable does, not just this feature. This is the one outcome that costs more than the charges themselves.

the expensive tailLow odds, high cost

The public screenshot

A charged creator posts in a group chat: "Benable took $62 from my card — where does it say they can do that?" With a terms page, one link answers the question and the thread dies. Without one, the answer is "a screen you saw once during signup," which reads as an admission.

reputationalOne screenshot away

The legal tail

Most saved cards will be debit cards, and charging a debit card without documented authorization touches federal consumer-protection rules for electronic transfers, plus state consumer-protection laws. At our size and charge frequency this is unlikely, but not zero — and it is exactly the kind of exposure a signed-off terms page removes.

smallest rungTail of the tail
§4

What needs to ship, concretely

Four items. The first two must be live before the first campaign that shows the card screen; the third ships with the feature; the fourth can follow.

1 · before launch

Publish the terms page

A one-page document, "Content Guarantee Terms": what the card backs, the exact conditions for a charge, the amount rule (the product cost shown at save, never more, no added fees), the final warning, the 7-day refund. A full draft exists; it needs Tony's legal pass, then a permanent URL.

2 · before launch

Put the agreement line on the card screen

One legible line next to the save button: "By saving your card, you agree to the Content Guarantee Terms," with the terms linked. Saving the card is the agreement — no checkbox needed (Tony's call, and the standard pattern for payment screens; it also covers the Apple Pay path). The "Learn more" sheet links to the same page.

3 · with launch

Record each agreement

When a creator saves a card, store what they agreed to: the wording shown, the amount, the date and time. That stored record — not the screen — is what wins a bank dispute later. Engineering shipped the identical logging pattern for SMS consent this week, so this is a small add.

4 · can follow

Tighten one privacy sentence

Extend "necessary to process your payment" with "or to keep a payment method on file." Rides the next Privacy Policy update, alongside the planned SMS wording.

Could we launch with none of this?Technically yes — showing the screen and saving cards breaks no rule, and if we never charge anyone the gap never surfaces. But every charge we did make would rest on screenshots, every dispute would be a coin flip, and our payment account would carry the risk unprotected. Since the page is already drafted and the remaining work is a legal pass, a link, and a log line, we recommend shipping items 1–3 before the first card-screen campaign.
§5

One more thing we checked: the 18+ question

Benable's Terms allow creators from age 16, and contracts with minors are voidable — a 17-year-old (or their parent) can undo a card agreement at will. So we explored whether the card step needs an age gate. Findings first, then the decision.

checkedStripe doesn't check age

Stripe validates the card, not the person: network checks, CVC and ZIP, a bank verification if the bank demands one. None of that involves age — Stripe's own 18+ rules bind the merchant (us), not cardholders. And teen debit cards are an entire banking category (Greenlight, Step, authorized-user cards on a parent's account), so a 16-year-old passes every check with a card that is genuinely theirs.

checkedNo law requires an age gate

There is no legal requirement to verify age before storing a payment card, and storing a minor's card is not illegal — minors hold and use cards everywhere. Age has exactly one legal effect here: contract capacity. An under-18's agreement is voidable, which only matters at the moment we actually charge them — and the whole design intent is to almost never charge anyone.

decidedNo birthdate collection

We don't collect birthdates today (verified: no such field anywhere in the backend) and we're keeping it that way — collecting less personal data is also the privacy-friendlier posture. Instead, three cheap layers: an 18+ line in the terms and on the card screen ("you must be 18 or older to save a card"), an ops rule (never charge anyone known or claimed to be under 18, and instantly refund if it surfaces after a charge), and the existing waiver path when we know a creator is a minor. Worst case under this setup is one refunded product cost.
