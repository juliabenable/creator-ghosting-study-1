Benable · card launch · legal wiring decision brief · Aug 20, 2026

Do we actually need the Content Guarantee Terms?

The card screen already explains the charge, and people tick a box. So this brief asks the honest version of the question: if we launch without a terms page and one day charge someone, what is the worst that can happen? Then it prices the fix against that worst case.

For: the terms conversation Verified against: ToS full text · live Privacy Policy · backend schema Companion: Content Guarantee Terms v1 draft
§1

The thing we're deciding, from the top

For anyone landing here cold — two definitions and one honest doubt.

the featureThe Content Guarantee

Benable guarantees brands that every collab ends in content. To back that, creators joining a gifted campaign save a credit or debit card when they sign up (the rev-4 "Credit or debit card" screen). Nothing is charged today, or ever on the happy path. A charge can only happen if a campaign closes with the product kept, nothing posted, and no reply from the creator — always after a final warning — and posting within 7 days of a charge refunds it in full. It's a commitment device against ghosting, not a revenue line: the plan is to almost never charge anyone.

the documentThe Guarantee Terms

A one-page legal document behind that screen — this feature's equivalent of benable.com/terms. The sheet's "Learn more" would link to it, and ticking the existing checkbox would count as agreeing to it. It states, in contract language, the charge conditions, the amount rule, the refund window, and the authorization to charge the saved card. A v1 is already drafted. The question is whether to wire it in.

the doubtWhy we're even asking

Because the screen already explains everything in plain English, people tick a box agreeing to it, and we barely intend to charge. So is a formal terms page real protection, or just legal ceremony? That's the question this brief prices.

§2

What we already have, and the one thing we don't

The protection is not zero today. It comes in three layers, and only one of them is missing.

the screenBuilt, and good

Rev 4 already does what most companies' lawyers beg for: it names the conditions ("product kept, nothing posted, no reply"), shows the exact amount, promises a final warning, and puts a checkbox under it. The screen is the consent. That is most of the protection, and it ships either way.

the contractSilent

Our Terms of Use never mention charging anyone's card, for anything. The only money language is "the Services are free but we may charge for them someday." So when a creator's bank asks "show us the terms the cardholder agreed to," there is no document to hand over — just a screenshot of an app screen that may have changed since.

the recordHalf-built

As of Aug 20 we log SMS consent properly (verbatim copy, version, timestamp, IP). The guarantee checkbox doesn't write a record yet. Pixels on a screen are ephemeral; a consent row with the amount in it is what you can produce two years later.

The point in one lineThe terms page doesn't create the consent — the screen does that. The terms page makes the consent provable, durable, and linkable when someone hostile asks about it.
§3

Charging without it: the worst that can happen

A severity ladder, honestly weighted. Remember the design intent: we barely ever charge, we warn first, one reply cancels, and posting within 7 days refunds. That intent shrinks every rung below.

Nothing happens

The overwhelming case. The card deters, the content ships or Katie waives, nobody is charged. A terms page changes nothing here.

likelihoodAlmost always

A charged creator disputes with their bank

People call their bank, not us — a chargeback often arrives with no warning, so "we'd have refunded anyway" never gets the chance. Stripe asks for evidence of the cardholder's agreement. Without terms + a logged consent, it's a coin flip at best: lose the $62 and a ~$15 dispute fee. Annoying, capped, survivable.

per chargeThe common bad path

The dispute ratio poisons the Stripe account

This is the rung that costs more than money. Dispute rates are measured against charge volume, and our charge volume is tiny — a small number of disputes can equal a scary percentage. Elevated ratios trigger monitoring programs and account review; the far end is a restricted account, which breaks the payments rail for everything, not just the guarantee. Same shape as the carrier risk in the SMS brief: the platform enforcer moves faster than any law.

the expensive tailLow odds, high cost

The group-chat thread

A charged creator posts the screenshot: "Benable took $62 — where does it say they can do that?" With a terms page, Katie answers with one link and the thread dies. Without it, the answer is "a screen you saw once in onboarding," which reads as an admission. This is E-30's skeptic test applied to the charge itself.

reputationalOne screenshot away

The legal tail

Most saved cards will be debit, and an unauthorized debit charge touches federal consumer protections (EFTA / Reg E), plus state consumer-protection statutes. Individually it's a $62 problem; as a pattern it's complaint-and-regulator territory. At our scale and charge frequency: unlikely, not zero. The ToS arbitration clause probably still reaches these disputes either way.

smallest rungTail of the tail

Minors footnote: none of this is fixed by a terms page. A charge to a 17-year-old is voidable with or without one — that's handled by the ops rule (never charge anyone known or claimed to be under 18; instant refund if it surfaces) plus the existing waiver path. No birthdate collection needed.

§4

The honest verdict

skip itDefensible, barely

Launching without terms is not illegal. If we near-never charge, and auto-refund anyone who pushes back, the realistic exposure is small: some coin-flip chargebacks, the ratio tail, and threads with no link to answer them.

What we'd be accepting: every dispute argued from screenshots, the Stripe account carrying the ratio risk unprotected, and the deterrent resting on copy we can't produce later.

add itRecommended

Because the price is an afternoon. The ToS already has an "Additional Terms" hook, so nothing in the main ToS changes and nobody re-accepts anything. The v1 draft is written. The consent-log pattern shipped this week for SMS.

What it buys: disputes answered with an evidence pack (terms version + amount + timestamp + IP) instead of a coin flip, the ratio tail defused by winning or deterring disputes, and every "where does it say that?" answered with one link.

So: do we need it?"Need" is the wrong word — it's not a legal prerequisite for launch. It's cheap insurance against the two outcomes that cost more than the charge itself (the Stripe account and the public thread), priced at roughly zero because the hook, the draft, and the logging pattern all exist already.

§6

One more thing we checked: the 18+ question

Benable's Terms allow creators from age 16, and contracts with minors are voidable — a 17-year-old (or their parent) can undo the guarantee agreement at will. So we explored whether the card step needs an age gate. Findings first, then the decision.

checkedStripe doesn't check age

Stripe validates the card, not the person: network checks, CVC and ZIP, a bank verification if the bank demands one. None of that involves age — Stripe's own 18+ rules bind the merchant (us), not cardholders. And teen debit cards are an entire banking category (Greenlight, Step, authorized-user cards on a parent's account), so a 16-year-old passes every check with a card that is genuinely theirs.

checkedNo law requires an age gate

There is no legal requirement to verify age before storing a payment card, and storing a minor's card is not illegal — minors hold and use cards everywhere. Age has exactly one legal effect here: contract capacity. An under-18's agreement is voidable, which only matters at the moment we actually charge them — and the whole design intent is to almost never charge anyone.

decidedNo birthdate collection

We don't collect birthdates today (verified: no such field anywhere in the backend) and we're keeping it that way — collecting less PII is also the privacy-friendlier posture. Instead, three cheap layers: an 18+ attestation line in the terms and on the card screen, an ops rule (never charge anyone known or claimed to be under 18, and instantly refund if it surfaces after a charge), and the existing waiver path when Katie knows a creator is a minor. Worst case under this setup is one refunded product cost. This closes edge case E-3 from the audit.
