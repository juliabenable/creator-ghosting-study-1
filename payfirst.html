Creator ghosting · the pay-first deep dive

The two platforms where creators pay out of pocket — their UI, and whether it works

Stack Influence makes creators buy the product with their own money and pays them back after a verified post. TikTok Shop sells "refundable samples" — pay now, refunded when your content produces sales. These are the only production systems with real creator money at risk, which makes them the ceiling for our much gentler card hold. Here's what their screens actually say, and the evidence on how well the mechanic performs.

01

The two models, step by step

who fronts the money, and what earns it back

Stack Influence micro-influencer gifting

1

Creator applies to a campaign in the Stack app (browse by category, "collabs that come to you").

$

Creator buys the product from the brand's own store (typically Amazon) with their own card, at full price.

3

Creator posts per the campaign requirements; Stack verifies the live post.

✓

Reimbursement lands via PayPal — product + shipping + fees. Their marketing calls it "100% cash back."

No post = no reimbursement. The creator simply ate the purchase; the brand is never charged. The stick isn't a penalty — it's your own money not coming back.

TikTok Shop refundable samples commerce sample program

1

Creator finds the product — the Get Sample sheet offers "Get free sample" (if eligible) or the paid path.

$

"Buy first, refund later" — the creator pays for the sample through normal checkout.

3

Content must perform: the refund unlocks when the creator's shoppable content hits the seller's target (the UI example: "generate 2 sales from content to get refund").

✓

Refund issued once the target is met inside the program window.

Miss the target = keep the product, eat the cost. Note it's stricter than ours in two ways: money moves upfront, and the exit isn't "post" — it's "post something that sells."
02

The UI — what the screens actually say

official App Store sets + TikTok Shop Academy documentation

Three things to notice. ① The mechanic is named plainly on the button — TikTok writes "Buy first, refund later" right on the sheet, Stack brands reimbursement as "100% cash back." Nobody hides the money mechanic in fine print, and both still recruit at scale. ② The commitment sentence appears at request time — TikTok's sample request screen carries the post-or-consequences line right above Submit, exactly where our card screen sits. ③ The states are a machine — expired, not approved, on-hold-30-days, get-it-again. Consequences are lifecycle states in the UI, not angry messages.

03

Does it work? The success evidence

scale · post rates · what creators say about paying first

Stack Influence — the verdict first: the pay-first model demonstrably works at their tier. Eight years of operation, six-figure creator counts, $17M+ reimbursed lifetime, three straight Inc. 5000 appearances (473% three-year revenue growth) — and creators rate them 4.6 on Trustpilot (542 reviews), 4.4 on the App Store, 4.2 on Play. Nano and micro creators accept fronting their own money in volume, when the alternative is no brand deals at all. The fine print below is where it gets instructive.

Scale · verified

Small company, big supply — the model recruits fine

Founded 2018, ~32 staff, just $1.3M raised — sustained by the model itself. Site claims 340–410k vetted creators (an Aug 2026 press release says "11M+"; ~27× the on-site number — treat as reach inflation). Entry bar is deliberately tiny: 100–200 followers, approved within 24h. "Stop DMing brands — collabs come to you."

Read: pay-first filters for creators with spare cash and no better offers — and at the nano tier, that pool is effectively bottomless. No acceptance-rate data is published anywhere.
stackinfluence.com / about / pricing · Crunchbase · Tracxn · Inc. 5000 press release (self-published)
Economics · verified

Brands literally pay only on completion

~$30–39 flat per completed post, $0 if the creator never posts — and the brand isn't billed reimbursement either: "influencers who do not complete a post will not receive a reimbursement, and you will not be charged." The whole business is priced on the no-post risk landing on the creator.

Gap worth knowing: they publish no completion-rate number at all. "100% guaranteed" describes the brand's bill, not the post rate.
stackinfluence.com/pricing · intercom.help/stackinfluence (cost + reimbursement articles)
Creator sentiment · verified

4.6 stars — with a telling 5–7% tail

Positives repeat one word: fast — "paid back almost immediately as soon as they post." The negative tail is almost entirely reimbursement failure: never reimbursed after purchase, partial reimbursement (taxes shorted), opaque "AI email" post rejections where the creator eats 100%, and a PayPal-only payout rail that permanently banned at least one creator's account.

Read: nobody complains that money is at risk — they complain when the platform's side of the trade wobbles. Trust in the release, not the stake, is the entire acceptance question.
Trustpilot 4.6/542 (via indexed snippets; page 403s) · App Store 4.4/389 · Google Play 4.2/182 · r/UGCcreators Jan 2025
Failure modes · verified

The float, and fraud flowing backwards

Creators carry product + shipping + tax for a multi-week float (posting "typically occurs a few weeks after receiving the product"); "instant" applies only to the moment after approval. And money-at-risk invites gaming in reverse: reimburse-then-return "double dipping" (keep the refund, return the product to Amazon) got bad enough that Stack shipped a zero-tolerance returns policy with screenshot-evidence investigations; one G2 brand reported high refund rates on $25k+ of spend.

Read: every pay-first sharp edge is something our hold doesn't have — no cash outflow, no float, no payout rail, no return to game. The card sits still unless everything else already failed.
intercom.help/stackinfluence returns-policy + reimbursement articles · G2 (39 reviews)

TikTok refundable samples — the verdict first: launched Nov 20, 2024 as a pay-first commitment device, and TikTok's own Feb–Mar 2025 analysis claims sellers see 25% higher ROI than free samples — against a free-sample baseline where agencies report fewer than ~30% of gifted creators post at all. Most telling: within five months TikTok tightened the mechanic. And the creator-sentiment tail ("refundable sample jail") maps exactly onto where the trust scaffolding thins.

Mechanics · verified

Full price upfront, refund on 1–3 sales, 120 days

Creator pays retail + shipping + tax at checkout. Seller sets the release target: 1–3 orders, default 1 (self-purchases never count). Window: 120 days from receipt (recommended target-by-90, so orders clear the 30-day return period). Refund is automatic — 5–10 business days to the original payment method once each qualifying order clears returns. Miss the target: keep the product, eat the cost.

Note the default: one sale. The platform keeps the release condition as low and legible as it can while still being about performance.
TikTok Shop Academy: Guide to Samples · How to use refundable samples · Creator T&C (Jul 28, 2026) · Seller T&C (May 14, 2026)
The pivot · verified

Apr 3, 2025: "post a video" stopped earning the refund

At launch, sellers could release refunds for content creation alone. Five months in, TikTok killed that: "refunds will only be issued when creators meet sales thresholds… Refunds for content creation alone will no longer be available." Creators would buy, post minimum-effort content, collect the refund.

Crucial nuance for us: TikTok's goal is sales, so post-as-release leaked. Our goal is the post itself — so post-as-release is exactly right for a gifted collab, and it's the achievable, creator-controlled trigger their creators wish they had.
Official "How to use refundable samples" changelog · Lindsey Gamble (Nov 20, 2024 launch coverage)
Trust scaffolding · verified

What it takes to make "hold my money" palatable

Automatic refunds (no claim step) · a dedicated progress-tracking page toward the target · "Fast Refunds" for proven creators, which TikTok funds by keeping the seller's commission — the platform literally pays to speed up trust · auto-refund if the product is delisted or the shop closes · coupon restored for reuse.

Read: the mechanic only survives wrapped in guarantees that the release is low, legible, and instant. Our equivalents: "$0 today," "expires the moment you post," Katie-manual-only, post-and-refund.

Sentiment + the baseline · mixed verification

"Refundable sample jail" — and why free samples needed fixing

Complaints cluster on three things: money stuck pending targets, the SG checkout toggle defaulting to ON, and new creators seeing only refundable offers (free samples require recent sales history — pay-first is the de-facto on-ramp). The baseline it fixes: agency-reported free-gifting post rates of 12–34%, with only 5–10% of content worth promoting (third-party, methodology unpublished).

Read: nobody riots about skin-in-the-game per se — they riot about defaults they didn't choose and money that feels stuck. Disclosure and release speed are the whole fight.

04

What this buys our commitment hold

the argument this page hands Tony
Four takeaways for the card-hold test
  • Our hold is the gentle end of a proven spectrum. Two production systems already put real creator money at risk — full purchase price, upfront — and recruit fine. A $0-today card that's only ever used on total silence is strictly milder than what thousands of creators already accept.
  • Name the mechanic on the button. TikTok writes "Buy first, refund later"; Stack writes "100% cash back." Plain naming didn't kill either program — which validates "Commitment hold" as a literal title over something euphemistic.
  • Reimbursement trust is the whole game. Stack's posters lead with "instant payments, no approvals, no holds" because the fear is never the rule — it's whether the platform honors its side. Our equivalents: "$0 today," "expires the moment you post," and the post-and-refund door if a charge ever fires.
  • States beat scolding. TikTok renders consequences as calm lifecycle states (expired · on hold 30 days · get it again). Our ladder should keep that grammar: the hold "kicks in" as a dated state change, never as a mood.
  • Post-as-release is our structural advantage. TikTok had to abandon content-based refunds because their goal is sales and low-effort posts leaked through. Our goal IS the post — so our release trigger ("post — even late — and it expires") is both achievable and un-gameable in exactly the way their creators wish theirs were. Where their money sits stuck for up to 120 days, ours never leaves the creator's account at all.
