What StubHub actually says — the voice Tony cited

"process, not personal," dissected

Their seller-penalty copy, move by move

  1. 1Flat conditional grammar, no adjectives. The page opens by listing the trigger events plainly — fail to deliver the tickets you sold, deliver wrong or invalid tickets, change a listing after sale. If X, then Y. No "unfortunately," no blame words.
  2. 2The failure is priced, precisely. Two named recovery paths — the cost of replacement tickets for the buyer, or a 40% deduction from the sale — plus a $25 administrative fee per order. Numbers, not vibes: you can compute your worst case before you sell.
  3. 3The money has a destination. Charges are framed as covering the cost of making the buyer whole (their FanProtect guarantee: the buyer gets comparable-or-better replacement tickets). It's restitution with a receipt, not a punishment.
  4. 4Collection is mechanical. The charge goes to the payment method on the seller's account, and unpaid amounts offset future payouts. Stated as plumbing, not threat.
  5. 5Agreed in advance. All of it lives in seller terms the seller accepted at listing time — which is why Tony feels "upset at the process, not at them" when it fires.
The one line worth quoting on the replacement path: penalties can include "a comparable replacement ticket (which can exceed the original ticket price)."StubHub UK seller support, fetched Aug 18, 2026 — full text at support.stubhub.co.uk, article 80001014186

What we borrow vs. soften

  1. ✓Borrow: if-then grammar, the priced worst case ($62, once), the money's destination ("covers the brand's product cost"), agreed-in-advance framing.
  2. ✓Borrow: mechanical collection language in the receipt email — timeline of facts, no adjectives.
  3. ~Soften: StubHub has no mercy door; we add post-and-refund (post within 7 days → full refund) and the always-an-exit line, because our relationship is repeat-collaboration, theirs is transactional.
  4. ~Soften: no administrative fee, ever — the charge is the product cost exactly, or it's nothing. A fee is where "process" starts smelling like "profit."
